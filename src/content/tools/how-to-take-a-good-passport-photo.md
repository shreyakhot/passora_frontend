## Why passport photos look bad

Most bad passport photos come down to a few things you can control:

- **The camera is too close.** Close-up photos make the middle of the face look bigger. A Rutgers study found that a selfie taken about 12 inches (30 cm) from the face makes the base of the nose look about 30% wider than a photo taken from 5 feet (1.5 m).
- **The light comes from one direction.** A single ceiling light throws shadows under the eyes and chin. A direct flash flattens the face and adds shine to the forehead and nose.
- **The expression is forced.** Told to keep a straight face, most people tense up, stare or raise their eyebrows.
- **There is only one try.** A rushed shot at a store counter catches you mid-blink or with your head tilted, and nobody offers to take it again.
- **Clothes blend into the background.** A white top against a white wall makes your face look washed out and your shoulders disappear.

None of these is a rule. They are habits of how passport photos are taken, and each one has a fix.

## Prepare before the photo

### Hair and makeup

Wear your hair the way you usually do. The US allows hair to go past the edges of the photo as long as your whole head is shown, but it should not cover your eyes or the outline of your face. The UK asks for no hair in front of your eyes.

Everyday makeup is fine, as long as you still look like yourself: the photo is used to recognise you. Matte products photograph better than shiny ones, and a little powder takes the shine off a forehead or nose. Skip anything that changes the shape of your face, and never add a filter or smoothing afterwards.

### Clothing vs backdrop

Choose a plain top in a colour that stands out from the background: a mid or dark colour against a US white or off-white background. Avoid white, very pale colours and busy patterns. The US does not allow uniforms, clothes that look like a uniform, or camouflage. A collar or neckline that shows a little of your neck and shoulders frames the face better than a high, bulky one.

### Glasses and glare

For a US passport, take your glasses off: they are not allowed unless you cannot remove them for medical reasons, with a signed note from your doctor. If you normally wear glasses, take them off a minute before the photo so the marks on your nose fade. Other countries, such as the UK, allow glasses if there is no glare and the frames do not cover your eyes, but taking them off is still the simplest way to avoid a rejection.

## Posing and expression tips

- **Sit or stand up straight**, with your shoulders level and square to the camera.
- **Push your chin very slightly forward**, not up. This tightens the jawline without tilting your head, which must stay level and face the camera.
- **Relax your face.** Breathe out, let your shoulders drop, and think of something pleasant just before the shot.
- **Smile only slightly, mouth closed.** The State Department's renewal guidance allows "a neutral facial expression or natural smile," and it says photos with unusual expressions or squinting will not be accepted. A small, closed-mouth smile is the safest version. The UK asks for a plain expression with your mouth closed.
- **Keep both eyes open and natural.** Do not widen them. Close your eyes for a second just before the photo, then open them, to avoid a blink.
- **Take several shots** if you can, and pick the best one.

## Lighting and camera distance

Light makes the biggest difference to how a passport photo looks:

- **Face a window** in daylight, or put two lamps at about 45 degrees on each side of the camera, so both sides of your face are lit evenly.
- **Turn the flash off** if you can. It adds shine and red eye.
- **Avoid a single overhead light**, which casts shadows under your eyes, nose and chin.
- **Stand about 2 feet (60 cm) from the wall**, so your shadow falls behind you and not on the background.

Then set the distance. The camera should be at eye level and about 4 to 5 feet (1.2 to 1.5 m) away, using the normal lens or a 2x zoom rather than moving closer. The photo can be cropped to size afterwards, by hand or with the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker). Have someone else take it: for an online renewal, the State Department says "Have someone else take your photo. No selfies." ## Funny, bad and ugly passport photos: what to avoid

Some photos that look funny are not just unflattering; they are refused. A real passport photo has to show your face clearly and naturally, because it is used to check that the passport is yours. Avoid:

| What happens | Why it is a problem |
| :--- | :--- |
| A big grin, a pout or raised eyebrows | Unusual expressions are not accepted |
| Squinting or half-closed eyes | Both eyes must be open |
| A tilted head or a turned face | You must face the camera directly |
| Shadows on the face or the wall | The face and background must be evenly lit |
| Glasses glare or red eye | The eyes must be clearly visible |
| A hat, hood or headphones | Not allowed, except religious or medical head coverings |
| A beauty filter or smoothing | Edited photos are refused |

The full list of what a passport photo must and must not show is in our [checklist of passport photo rules](/tool/passport-photo-requirements).

## Popular online tips, checked against the rules

Forums and travel blogs pass around the same handful of tips for a better passport photo. Here is how they fit the US rules:

| Tip | Allowed? |

| Smile slightly with your mouth closed | Yes |
| Push your chin forward a little | Yes, if your head stays level and faces the camera |
| Wear a dark, plain top | Yes, as long as it is not a uniform or camouflage |

| Wear light makeup | Yes, if you still look like yourself |

| Wear your glasses so the photo looks like you | No, for a US passport |
| Put the background in afterwards | No: take the photo against a plain wall |

## Retake or accept?

A US passport photo stays in your passport for up to ten years, and you cannot swap it in a passport that has already been issued: changing it means applying for a new passport. So it is worth a second try before you submit.

**Retake it** if any rule is broken: shadows, glare, closed eyes, hair over the eyes, a tilted head, or a head that is too big or too small in the frame. **Retake it if you can** when you simply do not like it. At home, try again as often as you like. At a store, look at the photo before you pay and ask for another shot if you need one. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I not look bad in my passport photo?

Stand 4 to 5 feet from the camera, face a window or two soft lights, wear a plain darker top, and relax with a slight closed-mouth smile. Most bad photos come from a camera that is too close and light from directly above.

### What should I wear for a passport photo?

A plain top in a mid or dark colour that contrasts with the white background. No uniforms, camouflage, hats or headphones, and no glasses for a US passport.

### Can I take my passport photo again if I do not like it?

Before you submit it, yes, as many times as you need. After the passport is issued, the only way to change the photo is to apply for a new passport.

### Why do I look different in my passport photo?

Usually because of the distance and light. A close camera exaggerates the nose and middle of the face, and flat or overhead light hides the shape of your face. A strict neutral expression also looks less like you than your normal face does.

### Is a perfect passport photo one that is edited?

No. Passport offices check for editing and refuse photos that have been smoothed, filtered or changed with software or AI. A perfect passport photo gets its look from preparation, light and distance, not from editing.
