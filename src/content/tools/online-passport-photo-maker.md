## What the AI passport photo maker does

Getting the size right is the hard part of a home-made passport photo. Each country sets a print size, a head size and a position for the eyes, and a photo that is a few millimetres off can be refused. The maker handles the measuring for you:

1. **Finds your face.** It locates your eyes, chin and the top of your head.
2. **Scales and centres.** It sizes your head to the range your country sets and centres it in the frame.
3. **Checks the background.** For most countries, it evens out the area behind you to a plain, light background if needed. For a US passport it uses **crop-only mode** by default: your background is checked but left exactly as you took it.
4. **Crops to size.** It crops the photo to the country's exact dimensions, for example 2×2 inches for a US passport or 35×45 mm for many European passports.
5. **Shows you the result.** You check the preview before paying. Nothing is charged if you do not like it.

After payment you get two files: a digital photo for online applications, and a 6×4″ (10×15 cm) sheet with several copies that any photo shop or kiosk can print.

## Before you upload: start with a good photo

The maker can fix size and framing. It cannot fix a blurry, dark or tilted photo, and some authorities check how much a photo has been changed. A good starting photo gives the best result and needs the least editing.

- **Plain wall behind you.** Stand about half a metre (2 feet) in front of a plain white or off-white wall. This keeps background changes to a minimum.
- **Even light from the front.** Face a window in daylight, or use two lamps either side of the camera. Avoid overhead light that throws shadows under your eyes.
- **Camera at eye level, about 1.2 m (4 feet) away.** Ask someone else to take the photo. Selfies at arm's length distort the face.
- **Neutral expression, eyes open, mouth closed.** Look straight at the camera.
- **No glasses, hats or headphones.** Religious or medical head coverings are allowed by many countries, usually with a signed statement.
- **No filters or beauty modes.** Turn off portrait mode, smoothing and HDR effects in your camera app.

## AI photo editing and official rules

Some passport authorities do not accept photos that have been digitally changed. The full list of what an authority checks is in our [passport photo rules checklist](/tool/passport-photo-requirements). The US State Department tells applicants: "Do not change your photo using computer software, phone apps or filters" and to "Submit the original, unchanged photo." Its guidance also says it checks photos for digital enhancement and alteration, including changes made with artificial intelligence.

What this means in practice:

- **For a US passport**, take your photo against a plain white or off-white wall in even light, so it needs only cropping and sizing. Do not use beauty filters, face reshaping or AI-generated backgrounds. When you choose the United States, the maker switches to crop-only mode: it measures, crops and sizes your photo without changing the background or adding to the frame. If your photo is framed too tightly to crop without adding background, it asks you to retake it from a little further away.
- **For other countries and visas**, rules differ. Some services crop the photo themselves after you upload it, and some ask for a photo-booth or studio code. Read the instructions of the authority you are applying to.
- **If in doubt, ask the authority.** No online tool, studio or retailer can promise that a photo will be accepted. The issuing authority makes that decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an AI passport photo accepted for a US passport?

The State Department asks for an original photo that has not been changed with software, apps, filters or artificial intelligence, and it checks for alteration. Cropping a photo to size is normal, but beauty filters, face changes and generated backgrounds are not acceptable. Take your photo against a plain white or off-white wall in even light, and check the [State Department photo page](https://travel.state.gov/en/passports/apply/help/photos.html) before you apply.

### Can I use a photo I already have?

Yes, if it was taken in the last six months, shows your full face looking straight at the camera, and is sharp and evenly lit. Group photos, selfies taken at arm's length and photos with filters rarely work well.

### Do I pay before I see the photo?

No. You see the processed photo first, and you pay only if you want to download it.

### Can I print the photo at home?

You can, on glossy or matte photo paper with a photo printer. Ordinary printer paper is not accepted for paper applications. Many people find it cheaper and more reliable to have the 6×4″ sheet printed at a photo kiosk.

### Is this an official government service?

No. This is an independent photo service and is not affiliated with any government, passport office, embassy or consulate. Always confirm the current photo rules with the authority that issues your document.
