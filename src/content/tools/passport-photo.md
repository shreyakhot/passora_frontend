## What a passport photo has to show

Passport photos are used by border officers and, increasingly, by face-matching systems at airports. That is why the rules are strict and why they look much the same everywhere. In almost every country, your photo must:

- **Be recent.** Usually taken in the last six months, so it looks like you now.
- **Show your full face, straight on.** Eyes open, mouth closed, a neutral expression, head not tilted.
- **Have a plain, light background.** No patterns, objects or shadows behind you.
- **Be evenly lit and sharp.** No shadows on your face, no glare, no blur or pixelation.
- **Be the right size,** with your head taking up a set share of the frame.
- **Be unedited.** No filters, beauty modes or retouching. Some authorities also check for digital changes.

Most countries also ask you to remove glasses, hats and anything that hides part of your face. Religious and medical head coverings are generally allowed, often with a signed statement. The full list, with the US rules as a worked example, is in our [passport photo rules checklist](/tool/passport-photo-requirements).

## Passport photo sizes around the world

There is no single passport photo size. The two you will see most often are:

| Size | Used by (examples) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **2 × 2 in (51 × 51 mm)** | United States passports and US visas |
| **35 × 45 mm** | The United Kingdom, Schengen countries, India and many others |
| **50 × 70 mm** | Canadian passports |

Your head must also fit a set range inside the frame. For a US passport that is 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm) from the bottom of your chin to the top of your hair. Our guide to [photo sizes compared across countries](/tool/passport-photo-size) covers pixel sizes for online forms and how to check the size of a photo you already have.

## Three ways to get a passport photo

| Option | Best for | Things to know |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Store or post office** | Getting prints the same day with no setup | Prices and quality vary by store; some locations need an appointment |
| **Professional photographer** | Babies, special requirements, or when you want a retake on the spot | Usually the most expensive option |
| **Online, from your own photo** | Saving a trip and choosing from several shots | You take the photo; the tool sizes it and you print the sheet or upload the file |

### In a store or post office

In the US, many pharmacies, shipping and print stores, and post offices that accept passport applications take passport photos while you wait. Our [guide to finding passport photos nearby](/tool/passport-photos-near-me) explains which types of places offer them and what to check before you go. If you are applying in person at an acceptance facility, ask whether it takes photos too, so you can do both in one visit.

### With a photographer

A studio photographer is a good choice for newborns and young children, for applicants who need help with posture or lighting, and for countries with unusual sizes. See our guide to [booking a professional studio photographer](/tool/passport-photographer-near-me).

### Online, from your own photo

You can take the photo at home with a phone and have it sized online. This is often the cheapest option and lets you retake the photo as many times as you like. You get a digital file for online applications and a print sheet for paper forms. Our [AI passport photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker) explains how it works and what to watch for with edited photos.

## Online applications and digital photos

More countries now accept or require a digital photo, for example online passport renewals in the US and online passport applications in the UK. Digital photos have their own rules for pixel size, file size and file type. Some services also crop the photo themselves after upload, so read the application site's instructions before you prepare the file. Our guide to [digital photo rules for online applications](/tool/digital-passport-photo) covers the common requirements.

## Common reasons photos are rejected

- Shadows on the face or the background
- Glasses, or glare on glasses
- Head too big, too small or off-centre
- A background that is not plain or light enough
- Blurry, grainy or pixelated prints, or prints on ordinary paper
- Filters, smoothing or other digital changes
- A photo that is older than allowed or no longer looks like you

Most of these are easy to avoid if you check the photo against your country's rules before you submit it. Our [tips for a photo that passes first time](/tool/how-to-take-a-good-passport-photo) go through each one.

## Visa, ID and other photos

Visa, residence permit, driving licence and ID card photos follow similar rules but not always the same size. A Schengen visa, for example, usually uses 35 × 45 mm, while a US visa uses 2 × 2 inches. Our [guide to visa photos by destination](/tool/visa-photo) explains how they differ.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a passport photo cost?

Prices vary a lot by place and country. Store and post office photos usually cost more than making one online from your own picture, and studio photographers usually cost the most. Our [price comparison of every option](/tool/passport-photo-cost) has the details.

### Can I take my own passport photo?

Yes, most countries accept a photo taken by a friend or family member, as long as it meets every rule. The US State Department lists "a friend or family member" as one way to get a photo. Selfies held at arm's length are usually rejected because they distort the face.

### Can I smile in a passport photo?

Most countries ask for a neutral expression with your mouth closed. The US asks you to face the camera "with a neutral expression, both eyes open and visible, and mouth closed."

### How recent does a passport photo have to be?

Most countries require a photo taken in the last six months. Check your country's rules, because some ask for a more recent photo, and you need a new one if your appearance has changed.

### Can I wear glasses in a passport photo?

Not for a US passport, unless you cannot remove them for medical reasons and include a signed statement from your doctor. Many other countries also ask you to remove glasses because of glare.
