## Rules for baby passport photos (US)

A baby's US passport photo must meet the same rules as anyone's, with one main exception: the eyes.

| Rule | What it means for a baby |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | 2 × 2 inches, head 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm) from chin to top of head |
| **Background** | Plain white or off-white, with no pattern, toys or blanket texture |
| **Eyes** | "It is acceptable if your infant's eyes are not entirely open." All other children must have their eyes open |
| **Nobody else** | "No other person can be in the photo," so no hands, arms or shoulders supporting the baby |
| **Face** | Full face visible, facing the camera, no shadows on the face |
| **Recent** | Taken in the last six months, so it shows how your baby looks now |
| **Unedited** | No filters or software changes, including removing a hand from the photo |

Other countries have their own rules for babies. Some require the eyes to be open at any age, so check the issuing authority's rules if your baby is applying for another country's passport or visa.

## How to photograph your baby

Both methods below support the baby's head without anyone appearing in the photo. Use daylight from a window in front of the baby, or two lamps either side of you, so there are no shadows.

### Laying baby on a plain sheet

1. Spread a plain white or off-white sheet on the floor or a bed, pulled tight so there are no creases.
2. Lay your baby on their back in the middle of the sheet, with the head straight.
3. Stand directly above, holding the camera parallel to the baby's face, not at an angle.
4. Make sure no shadow from you or the camera falls on the baby or the sheet.
5. Take plenty of photos, ideally when the baby is calm and alert, and keep the best one where the face is straight and fully visible.

### Car-seat trick

Cover a car seat with a plain white or off-white sheet and sit your baby in it, so the seat supports the head. Take the photo from straight in front, at the baby's eye level. Check that no straps, buckles or edges of the seat show around the face, and that the sheet behind the head is smooth and evenly lit.

### Eyes open vs asleep

For a US passport, a baby's eyes do not need to be fully open, so a drowsy photo can work. A photo of an alert baby looking at the camera is still the safest choice. For older children, the eyes must be open.

### Common problems

- **Hands in the frame:** retake the photo; do not crop or edit them out.
- **Head tilted to one side:** roll a small towel under the sheet to support the head, out of sight.
- **Shadows from the parent:** move the light, or shoot from slightly further away and crop.
- **Blanket texture showing:** use a smoother sheet, pulled flat.

Once you have a good photo, the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker) can measure and crop it to 2 × 2 inches. For a US passport it only crops and sizes the photo and leaves the background as you took it, so start with a clean white sheet.

## Newborn passport photos: how early?

A baby can get a US passport at any age, including a few days old, as long as the application can be made in person. The photo must show how the baby looks now and must be taken in the last six months.

Children under 16 cannot renew a passport by mail or online. Each new child passport is applied for in person with Form DS-11, usually with both parents or guardians present, or with consent on Form DS-3053 if one cannot attend. Babies change quickly, so take the photo close to the appointment date. Our guide to [photos for renewals and new applications](/tool/passport-application-photo) explains how each route works.

## Where to get infant passport photos

### Studios

A photographer used to babies can take the time to get a usable photo, with a proper backdrop and lighting. This is often the least stressful option for a newborn. See our guide to [choosing a professional photographer](/tool/passport-photographer-near-me).

### Retail photo counters

Some stores take passport photos of babies, including [Walgreens](/tool/walgreens-passport-photos). A busy counter can be hard with a newborn, so go at a quiet time and ask whether staff can photograph a baby lying down.

### At home or online

The white-sheet method above works well at home. You can then crop the photo yourself or use an online tool, and print it on photo paper. Our guide to [printing passport photos at home or in store](/tool/print-passport-photos) covers the print settings.

## Infant passport photo near me: what to ask

Before you go, phone the studio or store and ask:

1. Do you photograph babies who cannot sit up?
2. Can you photograph a baby lying down on a white sheet?
3. Can I come back for a retake if the photo is rejected?
4. Is there a quiet time of day you would recommend?

## Common reasons infant photos are rejected

- A parent's hand, arm or shoulder is visible
- Shadows on the face or behind the head
- The baby's head is turned or tilted
- A patterned blanket or car seat shows in the background
- The photo has been edited, for example to remove a hand

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do a baby's eyes have to be open in a US passport photo?

No. The State Department says it is acceptable if an infant's eyes are not entirely open. Older children must have their eyes open, and other countries may require open eyes for babies too.

### Can I hold my baby for the passport photo?

Not in the photo. No other person can appear, including hands supporting the baby. Lay the baby on a white sheet or use a car seat covered with a white sheet instead.

### How old does a baby have to be to get a passport?

There is no minimum age. Newborns can get a US passport, applied for in person with Form DS-11, using a photo taken in the last six months.

### How long is a baby's passport valid?

A US passport issued to a child under 16 is valid for five years. Because children's appearance changes, you will need a new photo for each application.
