## Europe

### UK

- **Size:** 45 mm high × 35 mm wide, with the head **29–34 mm** from chin to crown.
- **Background:** plain cream or light grey for printed photos; plain and light-coloured for digital ones.
- **Prints:** 2 identical printed photos for a paper form, taken in the **last month**.
- **Online:** one digital photo, at least 600 × 750 pixels and 50 KB to 10 MB.
- No glasses unless you have to wear them, and never sunglasses or tinted lenses.

### Ireland

- **Size:** at least 35 × 45 mm, at most 38 × 50 mm, showing your head to mid-torso with space around the head.
- **Background:** plain light grey, white or cream, with enough contrast.
- **Paper form:** 4 identical photos, no more than 6 months old; your witness signs and stamps the back of two.
- **Online:** a digital photo of at least 715 × 951 pixels, JPEG, no larger than 9 MB, not digitally enhanced. Irish citizens living in the US must apply online.
- Clear glasses are allowed if there is no glare.

### Germany

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the face **32–36 mm** high for adults and children aged 10 and over.
- **Background:** plain and light, ideally neutral grey.
- **In Germany:** since **May 1, 2025**, paper photos are no longer accepted. The photo is taken at the passport office, or a photographer sends it to the office digitally.
- **At German missions in the US:** printed 35 × 45 mm photos (portrait, no border) are still accepted until the missions have their own capture equipment. Germany's US mission site asks for two identical photos and says not to cut them yourself.

### France

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the head **32–36 mm** (70–80% of the photo) from the bottom of the chin to the top of the skull, not counting hair.
- **Background:** plain and light, such as light blue or light grey. **White is not allowed.**
- **Number:** one photo, from an approved professional photographer or photo booth. This also applies when applying at a French consulate in the US.
- Taken within 6 months; glasses are optional, but lenses must not be tinted.

### Italy

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the face covering **70–80%** of the photo from the chin to the forehead.
- **Background:** white, evenly lit.
- **Number:** one recent photo in Italy; the New York consulate asks for **two identical** photos.
- At the New York consulate, applicants over 12 can have the photo taken at the consulate.
- Taken within 6 months. The New York consulate says no glasses, so take them off.

### Spain

- **Size:** a small photo, **26 mm wide × 32 mm high** (Spain writes it as 32 × 26 mm), not 35 × 45 mm.
- **Background:** plain white, facing the camera.
- **Number:** one recent colour photo, also at Spanish consulates in the US.
- No dark glasses or clothing that hides your face.

### Poland

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the face filling **70–80%** of the photo.
- **Background:** white, evenly lit.
- **Number:** one recent colour photo, printed, which you bring when you apply in person.
- Taken no more than 6 months before applying; glasses are allowed if the eyes are clearly visible; lips closed.

### Greece

- **Size:** **40 × 60 mm**, with the head (from the base of the shoulders to the top of the hair) filling 70–75% of the photo.
- **Background:** grey.
- **In Greece:** the photo is now digital only. A photographer uploads it through the myPhoto service and you get a code.
- **Abroad:** you upload the photo to the Foreign Ministry's platform, have it taken at a consulate that has the equipment, or bring a print. The Greek embassy in the US and the national page give different rules on how recent the photo must be, so ask your consulate.
- Wear glasses if you usually do; neutral expression without a smile.

## Asia and Middle East

### China

- **Size:** 33 × 48 mm, with the head **28–33 mm** from chin to crown and 15–22 mm wide.
- **Background:** white; colour photo taken within 6 months.
- **From the US:** you apply in the China Consular app and upload a photo or take one in the app. The embassy says the uploaded photo is printed in the new passport, so you do not mail a print.

### Japan

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the head **32–36 mm** from the top of the head to the chin.
- **Background:** plain; white is recommended. Taken within 6 months.
- **Number:** one photo, or two to replace a lost or stolen passport.
- Edits that change your face, such as enlarging the eyes or slimming the face, get photos rejected.

### South Korea

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, with the head **32–36 mm** from crown to chin (23–36 mm for children aged 7 and under).
- **Background:** white. Do not wear white clothing; both ears must be visible.
- **In the US:** the Los Angeles consulate asks for one photo and has a free photo machine; the Chicago consulate also accepts the US 2 × 2 inch size.
- **Online reissue:** 413 × 531 pixels recommended, JPG, 500 KB or less.

**Korean visa photo:** the visa application form asks for a 35 × 45 mm colour photo taken within 6 months, on a white or off-white background. The Chicago consulate accepts 2 × 2 inch or 35 × 45 mm, printed on photo paper only.

### Iran

Iran's Foreign Ministry pages describe a **3 × 4 cm** photo on a plain white background, taken within 6 months, with women wearing a headscarf. Other Iranian missions ask for 4 × 6 cm, and the Interests Section in Washington asks for two photos that follow its own photo guideline. Check the guideline for the office handling your application before printing.

### Israel

- **In Israel:** the Population and Immigration Authority takes your photo at the office. Its specification is 35 × 45 mm, with the face 32–36 mm from chin to hairline, on an even, light background.
- **At Israeli consulates in the US:** two **2 × 2 inch** photos on a white background, taken within 3 months, without glasses and with hair behind the ears. Consulates in the US do not issue biometric passports; those are issued in Israel.

### Lebanon

- **Size:** 35 × 45 mm, colour, on a white background.
- **In Lebanon:** General Security asks for a new photo every time, signed and certified by the mayor.
- **In the US:** since October 1, 2026, biometric passport applications and renewals are made in person at the embassy, where your photo is taken. The embassy also says it can adjust photo dimensions for you.

### Saudi Arabia

The passports directorate (Jawazat) asks for colour **4 × 6 cm** photos on a white background, not instant prints or scans; its procedures list two recent photos. Online renewal from abroad through the Foreign Ministry asks you to attach a photo of 2 MB or less. Ask the Saudi embassy for any US-specific rules.

### Syria

The Syrian Foreign Ministry asks for **two recent colour photos, 4 × 4 cm, on a white background, wearing dark clothing**. Scanned photos are refused; no glasses; shoulders must show. Appointments are booked through the MOFA SY app.

### Nepal

Nepal now issues e-passports, and the photo is **taken at the appointment**, both in Nepal and at the embassy in Washington. You do not bring prints. On the day:

- Remove glasses, jewellery, make-up and tika.
- Tie hair back so your forehead and both ears show.
- Wear a dark collared top that contrasts with the white background.

### Turkey

- **Size:** **50 × 60 mm** biometric photo, with the face 32–36 mm (70–80% of the photo), on a plain white background, taken within 6 months.
- **In the US:** the Los Angeles consulate accepts the US **2 × 2 inch** size too, and asks for one biometric photo that you bring.
- Glasses: consulates differ (New York allows prescription glasses, Los Angeles says not to wear them), so take them off.

**Turkish visa photo:** a standard Turkish e-Visa does not ask for a photo. Sticker visas from US consulates ask for one 2 × 2 inch photo.

## Africa and Americas

### Nigeria

The Nigeria Immigration Service **captures your photo and biometrics** in person, at an immigration office in Nigeria or at the embassy and consulates abroad. The online form asks you to upload an ICAO-compliant picture on a **white background**. The embassy in Washington also asks for two passport-size photos, and the Atlanta consulate describes a 2 × 2 inch photo. Since November 2024, people in the US can also renew through an app that captures the face and fingerprints remotely.

### Egypt

Egyptian sources disagree. The Foreign Ministry asks for **four 4 × 6 cm** photos on a white background; the embassy in Washington asks for **three 2 × 2 inch** photos on a white background. Use the checklist of the office where you apply. Apply in person; a parent must attend for applicants under 15.

### Ethiopia

Ethiopia now issues electronic passports, which need **fingerprints given in person** at an immigration branch or at the embassy. Renewal asks for one passport-size photo. For online renewal through the embassy's app, the photo must be under 2.5 MB. Ask the embassy for the printed size if you need one; no official size was published on the pages checked.

### South Africa

- **In South Africa:** Home Affairs offices with automated systems capture your photo digitally, so you do not need prints there.
- **From the US:** applications are made in person through VFS Global, and the embassy's February 2026 checklist asks for **4 photos** (mouth closed, no glasses). Mailed applications are not processed from March 1, 2026.
- **Size:** a Home Affairs specification says 35 × 45 mm, with the face filling 70–80% of the photo on a light grey or plain cream background, taken within a month. The embassy's passport page asks for 2 × 2 inch photos. Ask the embassy which size it currently accepts.

### Brazil

- **In Brazil:** the Federal Police photographs every applicant aged 5 and over during the appointment. Only children under 5 bring a photo (5 × 7 cm).
- **At Brazilian consulates in the US:** one **2 × 2 inch** photo on a white background, the same as a US passport photo. No glasses and no digitally edited photos. The New York consulate says to glue the photo on the form, even over the barcode.
- **Foto 3x4:** a 3 × 4 cm photo is the usual size for Brazilian ID cards and many other documents. It is not the passport size.

## International passport photos abroad

If you are applying for your own country's passport from the US:

1. **Download your consulate's checklist.** It may differ from the rules at home: Israel and Brazil ask for 2 × 2 inch photos at their US consulates, and some Turkish and Korean consulates accept them.
2. **Check whether the consulate takes the photo.** Nepal, Lebanon and Nigeria photograph you at the appointment; China takes the photo through its app.
3. **Ask a store or studio for the size by name**, in millimetres, and bring the checklist. Many US store counters print only 2 × 2 inch photos by default. Our guide to [places that take visa photos](/tool/visa-photo-near-me) lists chains that make photos for other countries.
4. **Watch the background colour.** France forbids white, Greece wants grey, and the UK wants cream or light grey, while most others want white.

You can also take the photo at home against a plain wall in the right colour and use the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker), which crops it to the size for the country you choose. Check first that your country accepts photos you take yourself: France requires a photo from an approved professional or photo booth, and inside Germany and Greece the photo must come from the passport office or a registered photographer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a standard international passport photo size?

No. 35 × 45 mm is the most common size, used across most of Europe and much of Asia, but the US uses 2 × 2 inches, Canada 50 × 70 mm, China 33 × 48 mm and Turkey 50 × 60 mm.

### What size is a UK passport photo?

45 mm high by 35 mm wide, with the head 29–34 mm from chin to crown, on a plain cream or light grey background.

### What size is a German passport photo?

35 × 45 mm, with the face 32–36 mm high. In Germany, paper photos are no longer accepted; the photo is taken at the office or sent digitally by a photographer.

### What size is a Chinese passport photo?

33 × 48 mm, with the head 28–33 mm from chin to crown, on a white background. From the US, you upload it in the China Consular app.

### Can I use a US 2 × 2 photo for another country's passport?

Only if that country's consulate accepts it. Brazil, Israel and some Turkish and Korean consulates in the US do; most European countries need 35 × 45 mm.
