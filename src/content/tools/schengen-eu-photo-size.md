## Schengen photo requirements

The European Commission asks for one photo that meets ICAO standards with every Schengen visa application. National consulates add their own wording, but the core rules match.

### Size

| Unit | Schengen visa photo |
| :--- | :--- |
| Millimetres | 35 × 45 (width × height) |
| Centimetres | 3.5 × 4.5 |
| Inches | About 1.38 × 1.77 |
| Pixels at 300 dpi | 413 × 531 |

The Commission's guidance allows a width of 35–40 mm, and France gives the same range. Germany and the Netherlands ask for 35 × 45 mm. A 35 × 45 mm photo fits every consulate's rules. For conversions and how the format compares with other sizes, see our [guide to the 35 × 45 mm format](/tool/35x45mm-passport-photo).

### Head size

The face must take up **70–80% of the photo's height**. In a 35 × 45 mm photo that is **32–36 mm**.

- Germany measures from the bottom of the chin to the crown (the top of the head, not the top of the hair). It rejects a photo only if the face is under 27 mm or over 40 mm.
- France measures from the chin to the forehead, excluding hair.
- For children aged 9 and under, Germany allows a smaller face: 22–36 mm.

### Background

The Commission's guidance and France ask for a **plain, light-coloured background**. The Netherlands accepts **white or light-coloured**. Whatever the shade, it must be:

- One even colour, with no pattern or objects
- Free of shadows, including the shadow of your head
- Light enough that your face and hair stand out clearly

Check your consulate's checklist in case it names a particular shade.

### Biometric standard

A Schengen visa photo is a biometric photo. It is used to check your identity against the Visa Information System (VIS), which stores your photo and fingerprints. Biometric means:

- Facing the camera straight on, with a neutral expression and mouth closed
- Eyes open and clearly visible, with no glare on glasses and no tinted lenses
- Even lighting, sharp focus and natural skin tones
- Taken in the **last 6 months**
- No editing beyond cropping

Germany warns that photos that break these rules can stop facial recognition from working, which is why they are rejected. Our [guide to biometric photos](/tool/biometric-passport-photo) explains the standard in more detail.

## EU passport photo size

Most EU countries use the same **35 × 45 mm** format for their own passports. The head size and background can still differ. Two examples:

| Country | Passport photo | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Netherlands | 35 × 45 mm | Own face-size rule for passports and ID cards, different from the visa rule |
| Ireland | 35 × 45 mm minimum, 38 × 50 mm maximum | Plain light grey, white or cream background |

For a citizen's passport, follow that country's own passport rules rather than the visa rules above. Our [list of photo sizes for every country](/tool/passport-photo-size-by-country) covers more countries.

## Schengen visa photo rules at VFS and embassies

You apply for a Schengen visa at the consulate or through an external service provider, such as VFS Global, that it works with. Either way:

- **The photo may be taken for you.** When you give your fingerprints in person, the Commission allows the photo to be taken on the spot. The Netherlands, for example, says applicants who use its external service provider do not need to bring a photo.
- **Some centres still want a print.** Others ask you to bring one or two photos for the form. The appointment confirmation or the centre's checklist says which.
- **Fingerprints are reused.** If you gave fingerprints for a Schengen visa within the last 59 months, you usually do not give them again, so you may need to bring a printed photo.
- **Long-stay (national) visas** follow the country's own rules. France asks for three photos for long-stay visas.

Read the checklist for your visa type on the consulate or VFS site before your appointment, and bring the number of photos it asks for.

## Schengen visa photos near me

Photo shops, pharmacies and print shops that make 35 × 45 mm photos can make a Schengen visa photo. Ask for:

1. **35 × 45 mm**, with the face 32–36 mm from chin to crown
2. The background colour your consulate asks for (white or light-coloured)
3. Two prints, or more if your checklist says so

In the US, several chains say they make photos for European countries; see our guide to [places that take visa photos](/tool/visa-photo-near-me). If the centre takes the photo at your appointment, you may not need one at all. To make one yourself, take it against a plain light wall and crop it to 35 × 45 mm with the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker).

## Foto para visa Schengen (Spanish)

Un resumen de los requisitos de la foto para visa Schengen:

- **Tamaño:** 35 × 45 mm (3,5 × 4,5 cm).
- **Rostro:** debe ocupar entre el 70 % y el 80 % de la foto, es decir, de 32 a 36 mm desde la barbilla hasta la parte superior de la cabeza.
- **Fondo:** liso y claro (blanco o de un color claro, según el consulado), sin sombras.
- **Expresión:** neutra, mirando de frente a la cámara, con los ojos abiertos y la boca cerrada.
- **Antigüedad:** tomada en los últimos 6 meses, sin retoques.

Consulte la lista de requisitos del consulado o del centro de solicitud de visados antes de su cita. En muchos centros se toma la foto durante la cita.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the photo size for a Schengen visa in cm?

3.5 × 4.5 cm, which is 35 × 45 mm. Some consulates also accept photos up to 4 cm wide.

### Can I use a US passport photo for a Schengen visa?

No. A US passport photo is a 2 × 2 inch square. Schengen visas need a 35 × 45 mm portrait photo with a larger face, so make a new photo from the original rather than trimming the square one.

### Is a white background allowed for a Schengen visa photo?

Usually. The Netherlands accepts white or light-coloured backgrounds, and the Commission asks for a plain, light-coloured one. Check your consulate's checklist in case it names a shade.

### Does VFS take the photo for a Schengen visa?

Many visa centres take a digital photo when they take your fingerprints, but not all of them, and some still ask for a printed photo. Check the checklist for your consulate and visa type.

### Can I wear glasses in a Schengen visa photo?

The ICAO standard allows clear glasses if your eyes are fully visible and there is no glare. Tinted lenses are not allowed. Taking them off is the safest choice.
