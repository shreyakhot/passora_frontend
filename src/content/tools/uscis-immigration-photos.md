## USCIS photo requirements

The current instructions for Form I-485 (green card) and Form I-765 (work permit) use the same wording. Each photo must be:

| Rule | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| Number | 2 identical colour photos, taken recently |
| Size | 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), full face, frontal view |
| Head height | 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm), from the top of the hair to the bottom of the chin |
| Eye height | 1 1/8 to 1 3/8 inches (29–35 mm), from the eyes to the bottom of the photo |
| Background | White to off-white |
| Paper | Thin paper with a glossy finish |
| Finish | Unmounted and unretouched |
| Head covering | Head bare, unless you wear headwear required by your religion |
| Back of the photo | Your name and A-Number (if you have one), printed lightly in pencil or felt pen |

"Unretouched" matters: do not use a photo with an edited background, smoothed skin or filters.

### Green card photos

With **Form I-485** (application to register permanent residence or adjust status), send the two photos described above. The USCIS Policy Manual adds that they **must have been taken within 30 days of filing**, which is stricter than the 6 months allowed for a passport photo.

The photo printed on your green card is not one of these prints. USCIS takes a new photo at your **biometrics appointment** at an Application Support Center. Since December 2025, Forms I-485 and I-90 (replacing a green card) always require new biometrics, including a new photograph.

### Citizenship photos

Most people applying for naturalization with **Form N-400** do not send photos, because USCIS takes your photo at the biometrics appointment. The N-400 instructions ask for **two identical colour passport-style photos** only from certain applicants who live outside the United States, such as some members of the military and their spouses. USCIS also says it may ask for physical photos after you file; if it does, it sends instructions.

Forms N-400 and N-600 (certificate of citizenship) always require new biometrics, including a new photograph.

Looking for Canadian citizenship photos instead? Those use a different size; see our [guide to Canadian photo rules](/tool/canadian-passport-photos).

### EAD, OPT and STEM OPT photo requirements

**Form I-765** (application for employment authorization, the EAD or work permit) asks for the same two photos, with your name and A-Number on the back. That includes F-1 students applying on paper for OPT or for the 24-month STEM OPT extension, which also uses Form I-765.

If you file online, the form asks you to upload a photo instead of mailing prints. USCIS's online filing guidance for OPT asked for one passport-style photo in JPEG or PNG that meets the State Department's passport photo rules. Follow the instructions in your online account.

For an EAD, USCIS may reuse a photo from an earlier biometrics appointment if no more than **3 years** have passed since it was taken. It can still ask for a new one.

## Immigration photo size

| Measurement | Inches | Millimetres | Pixels at 300 dpi |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Photo | 2 × 2 | 51 × 51 | 600 × 600 |
| Head (hair to chin) | 1 to 1 3/8 | 25–35 | 300–413 |
| Eyes (to the bottom edge) | 1 1/8 to 1 3/8 | 29–35 | 338–413 |

This is the same square as a US passport photo. Our [guide to 2 × 2 inch photos](/tool/2x2-passport-photo) shows where the head sits in the square and how to print one.

**Diversity Visa (green card lottery) entries** use a digital photo instead: a square JPEG of 600 × 600 pixels, no more than 240 kB, taken in the last 6 months, with the head 50–69% of the image height. The State Department says a manipulated photo, or reusing last year's photo, can disqualify an entry. Immigrant visa interviews at an embassy follow the State Department's visa photo rules; see our [guide to visa photo rules](/tool/visa-photo).

## Immigration photos near me

Any store that takes US passport photos can take immigration photos, because the size is the same. Walmart, for example, says its 2 × 2 prints are suitable for green cards as well as passports. When you go:

1. Say the photos are for a **USCIS form** and name it (I-485, I-765 or N-400).
2. Ask for **two identical prints** on **glossy photo paper**.
3. Make sure the background is plain white or off-white, with no shadows.
4. Do not let anyone trim, laminate or mount the photos.
5. At home, write your name and A-Number lightly on the back in pencil or felt pen.

For a green card, time the visit so the photos are no more than 30 days old when USCIS receives your application. To compare nearby stores, prices and digital copies, see our guide to [places that take passport photos nearby](/tool/passport-photos-near-me).

## Passport photos for immigration

A US passport photo and a USCIS photo use the same 2 × 2 inch frame and head size, so a recent passport photo often works. Check these differences first:

| | US passport | USCIS forms (I-485, I-765) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Number | 1 | 2 identical |
| How recent | Last 6 months | "Recently"; within 30 days of filing for I-485 |
| Paper | Matte or glossy photo paper | Thin paper, glossy finish |
| Back of the photo | Not required | Name and A-Number in pencil or felt pen |

The State Department no longer allows glasses in passport photos. The USCIS instructions do not mention glasses, so take them off to be safe. Our [checklist of passport photo rules](/tool/passport-photo-requirements) covers expression, lighting and clothing.

To make the photos yourself, take one against a plain white or off-white wall and use the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker). For US photos it crops and sizes the image without editing it by default, so the photo stays unretouched. Print two copies on glossy photo paper.

## Picture of a green card vs application photo

These are two different photos:

- **The application photos** are the two 2 × 2 inch prints you mail with Form I-485 or I-765. They go into your file.
- **The photo on your green card** is taken by USCIS at your biometrics appointment, along with your fingerprints and signature. You do not supply it, and you cannot choose it.

If an application asks for a copy of your green card, it means a photocopy of the card, front and back, not a new photo of you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What size is a USCIS immigration photo?

2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm), with the head 1 to 1 3/8 inches from the top of the hair to the bottom of the chin, on a white to off-white background.

### How many photos do I need for a green card application?

Two identical colour photos with Form I-485, taken within 30 days of filing, on thin glossy paper.

### Do I need photos for the N-400 citizenship application?

Usually not. USCIS takes your photo at the biometrics appointment. Only certain applicants living outside the United States must send two photos, and USCIS may request photos later.

### What do I write on the back of USCIS photos?

Your name and A-Number, if you have one, printed lightly in pencil or felt pen.

### Can I use a passport photo for my EAD or green card?

Yes, if it meets the USCIS rules: 2 × 2 inches, two identical glossy prints, recent enough (within 30 days for a green card application), and not retouched.
