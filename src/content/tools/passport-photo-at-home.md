## Can you take your own passport photo?

Yes, for a US passport. The State Department lists "a friend or family member" alongside acceptance facilities and photo businesses as places to get your photo. What matters is the result, not who took it: the photo must meet every rule, and it must not be changed with software, apps, filters or AI.

Other countries have their own rules. Canada, for example, requires passport photos to be taken by a commercial photographer, and the UK asks for a photo code from a booth or shop for some applications. Check the issuing authority's rules before you start.

## What you need

### Backdrop

A plain **white or off-white** wall, door or sheet, with no pattern, texture, shadows or objects. A smooth painted wall is easiest. If you use a sheet, pull it tight so there are no creases, and stand a short distance in front of it so you do not cast a shadow on it.

### Lighting

Soft, even light on both sides of your face:

- **Best:** daylight from a window in front of you, not behind you
- **Indoors at night:** two lamps, one either side of the camera, at about head height
- **Avoid:** a single overhead bulb, direct sun, and flash that creates glare or red eye

Shadows on the face or behind the head are one of the most common reasons a home photo is rejected.

### Camera or tripod

Any phone or camera that takes a sharp photo will do. Keep it at **your eye level**, held level, not tilted up or down. A tripod or a pile of books helps keep it steady and straight. Our guide to [taking the photo with your phone](/tool/take-passport-photo-with-phone) covers phone settings in detail.

### A helper

Ask someone to take the photo. The State Department's guidance assumes someone else takes it and that the camera is several feet away, which is hard to do on your own. If you are alone, use a tripod and the camera's timer instead of holding it at arm's length.

## How to take a passport photo at home, step by step

1. **Set up the background.** Stand a short step in front of a plain white or off-white wall, with nothing else in the frame.
2. **Arrange the light.** Face a window or put a lamp either side of the camera, so there are no shadows on your face or behind you.
3. **Get ready.** Take off glasses, hats and headphones. Wear everyday clothes, not a uniform, and keep your hair away from your face. A religious or medical head covering is allowed with a signed statement, as long as your full face is visible.
4. **Position the camera.** Your helper stands several feet away, with the camera at your eye level and pointing straight at you.
5. **Frame the shot.** Include your head and the top of your shoulders, with space above your head and on both sides. Leave room to crop; do not fill the frame with your face.
6. **Pose.** Face the camera directly, both eyes open, mouth closed, with a neutral expression and no teeth showing. Keep your head straight, not tilted.
7. **Take several photos.** Check each one at full size for sharpness, shadows and closed eyes, and keep the best.

## Crop and size it correctly

A US passport photo is **2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)**, with the head **1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm)** from the chin to the top of the hair. Resizing the whole photo is not enough: it has to be cropped so the head fills the right part of the square.

You can crop by hand in a photo editor, using a 1:1 ratio and measuring the head (our [settings for Photoshop, GIMP and Word](/tool/passport-photo-editing-photoshop-word) show how), or use the [online photo maker](/tool/online-passport-photo-maker). For a US passport it measures, crops and sizes your photo without changing your face or background. That is why a clean wall and good light matter: the photo is used as you took it.

Cropping is allowed. Changing what the photo shows is not: no smoothing skin, removing shadows, whitening the background or editing out stray hair.

## Print or upload your photo

- **Paper application (DS-11 or DS-82):** you need a print. Lay two or more photos on a 4 × 6 inch sheet and print it on photo paper at home or at a kiosk. Our guide to [printing passport photos at home or in store](/tool/print-passport-photos) covers the layout and printer settings.
- **Online renewal:** you upload a digital file instead. Our guide to [preparing a digital photo for your application](/tool/digital-passport-photo) lists the file types and sizes accepted.

## Mistakes that get DIY photos rejected

- Shadows on the face or a shadow of your head on the wall
- A grey, cream-coloured or textured background instead of white or off-white
- The camera held too close, which distorts the face, or too far, which makes the head too small
- Head tilted, or not facing the camera
- Glasses on, or glare on the face from flash
- A blurry or grainy photo, often from low light
- Filters, beauty mode or edits to the face or background
- Wrong head size after cropping

## Check your photo before you submit

Hold the finished photo against this list:

- 2 × 2 inches, head 1 to 1 3/8 inches, chin to top of hair
- Plain white or off-white background with no shadows
- Eyes open and visible, facing the camera, no glasses
- Sharp, in colour, taken in the last six months
- Unedited, apart from cropping

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a passport photo taken at home accepted?

Yes, for a US passport, if it meets every rule. The State Department accepts photos taken by a friend or family member. It rejects photos that have been edited with software, apps, filters or AI.

### Can I take a selfie for my passport photo?

It is better not to. A selfie at arm's length distorts the face and is often tilted. Have someone take the photo, or use a tripod and the timer.

### What colour wall do I need?

Plain white or off-white, with no pattern, texture or shadows. Grey, cream and patterned walls are common reasons for rejection.

### Can I wear a hat or headscarf?

Not a hat. A head covering worn daily in public for religious reasons is allowed with a signed statement saying so, and a medical one with a signed statement from your doctor. Either way, your full face must be visible with no shadows.
