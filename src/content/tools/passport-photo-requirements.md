## US passport photo requirements at a glance

These are the US State Department's rules, quoted where the wording matters. Other countries follow the same principles with different numbers. See the section on other countries below.

| Requirement | US rule |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Number of photos** | One colour photo |
| **Age of photo** | "Taken in the last 6 months" |
| **Size** | 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) |
| **Head size** | 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm), chin to top of head |
| **Background** | "White or off-white, free of shadows, and plain without texture, objects, or lines" |
| **Expression** | "Neutral expression, both eyes open and visible, and mouth closed" |
| **Glasses** | Not allowed, unless a signed doctor's statement explains a medical need |
| **Head coverings** | Not allowed, except religious or medical ones with a signed statement |
| **Clothing** | No uniforms, clothing that looks like a uniform, or camouflage |
| **Print** | Matte or glossy photo-quality paper; not blurry, grainy or pixelated; no photocopies or scans |
| **Edits** | "Do not change your photo using computer software, phone apps or filters" |

## The checklist, rule by rule

### 1. Size and framing

- The photo is the exact print size for your country: 2 × 2 inches for the US.
- Your head is centred, facing the camera, and fills the required height range.
- Your full head, from the top of your hair to your chin, and the tops of your shoulders are in the frame.

Measuring is where most home-made photos fail. Our guide to [passport photo dimensions in inches, mm and pixels](/tool/passport-photo-size) shows how to check a print or a file.

### 2. Background

- Plain white or off-white for the US. Many other countries ask for a plain light grey, cream or white background.
- No shadows from your head or shoulders.
- No patterns, texture, doors, furniture or other people behind you.

Stand about half a metre (2 feet) in front of the wall so your shadow falls behind you, not on the wall.

### 3. Face and expression

- Look straight at the camera with your head level, not tilted.
- Neutral expression, both eyes open, mouth closed.
- Hair should not cover your eyes or the outline of your face.

### 4. Glasses, hats and accessories

- **Glasses:** remove them, including tinted and prescription glasses. If you cannot remove them for medical reasons, the US asks for a signed statement from your doctor.
- **Hats and head coverings:** remove them unless worn daily for religious or medical reasons. In that case, include a signed statement, and make sure the covering does not hide your face or cast shadows.
- **Face coverings and masks:** remove them. Your full face must be visible.
- **Headphones and earbuds:** remove them.
- **Jewellery and piercings:** generally allowed if they do not hide your face.

### 5. Clothing

- Wear normal everyday clothes. A darker top stands out better against a white background.
- No uniforms, clothes that look like a uniform, or camouflage.

### 6. Lighting and image quality

- Even light on both sides of your face, with no shadows and no glare.
- Sharp focus, true colours, no red-eye.
- Not blurry, grainy or pixelated.

### 7. Printing

- Printed on matte or glossy photo-quality paper.
- Not a photocopy, a home print on ordinary paper, or a scan of an older photo.
- No creases, marks or holes.

### 8. No digital changes

The State Department says: "Do not change your photo using computer software, phone apps or filters," and "Submit the original, unchanged photo." It also says it checks photos for digital enhancement and alteration, and its guidance names artificial intelligence. That rules out:

- Beauty filters, skin smoothing and face reshaping
- Removing or replacing the background with an app or AI tool
- Colour filters and heavy retouching

Cropping the photo to the correct size is part of producing a passport photo. Changing how you or the background look is not. If your background is wrong, take the photo again against a plain wall rather than editing it.

## Children and babies

The same rules apply to children and babies, with some practical allowances. Only the child may be in the photo. No parent's hands or arms should be visible, and no toys or dummies. For a newborn, the photo can be taken with the baby lying on a plain white sheet. Babies' eyes do not always have to be fully open, but check your country's rules. Our guide to [photos for babies and newborns](/tool/infant-newborn-passport-photo) covers the technique.

## How other countries differ

The principles above are shared by most countries, because most follow the international standard for machine-readable passports. The details differ:

| Detail | What varies |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | 2 × 2 in (US), 35 × 45 mm (UK, Schengen countries, India), 50 × 70 mm (Canada) and others |
| **Background colour** | White in some countries, light grey or cream in others |
| **Head size** | Each country sets its own range |
| **Online applications** | Some accept your own digital photo; others need a code from an approved photo booth or studio |
| **Back of the photo** | Canada, for example, asks the photographer to add details to the back of one photo |

Visa photos follow the rules of the country you are visiting, not your own. Check our [guide to visa photos by destination](/tool/visa-photo), and always confirm with the issuing authority before you apply.

## Before you submit: a final check

1. Is the photo less than six months old, and does it still look like you?
2. Is it exactly the right size, with your head in the required range?
3. Is the background plain, light and free of shadows?
4. Are you facing the camera with a neutral expression, eyes open, and no glasses?
5. Is it sharp, evenly lit and printed on photo paper, or does the file meet the online upload rules?
6. Is it free of filters and edits?

If any answer is no, retake the photo. Our [tips for a photo that passes first time](/tool/how-to-take-a-good-passport-photo) cover lighting and setup.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many photos are required for a US passport?

One. The State Department asks for one colour photo, taken in the last six months, with your application. Some other applications, such as many immigration forms, ask for two.

### Can my passport photo have a grey or blue background?

Not for a US passport, which requires a white or off-white background. Some other countries accept light grey or cream. A blue or coloured background is rarely accepted for passports.

### Can I wear makeup in a passport photo?

Yes, everyday makeup is fine as long as you still look like yourself. Avoid anything that changes the shape or features of your face, and do not use beauty filters or smoothing on the photo.

### Can I wear a hijab, turban or other religious head covering?

Yes. Most countries, including the US, accept religious head coverings worn daily. The US asks for a signed statement, and the covering must not hide your face or cast shadows on it.

### What happens if my photo does not meet the requirements?

Your application is usually delayed while the passport office asks for a new photo. Checking the photo against this list before you submit is the easiest way to avoid that.
