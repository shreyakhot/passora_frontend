## When a professional photographer is worth it

For most adults applying for a US passport, a store photo counter or a photo taken at home works fine. A studio earns its higher price when getting it wrong would cost you time or another trip:

| Situation | Why a studio helps |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Babies and toddlers** | Patience, a proper backdrop and several attempts until the eyes are open and nobody else is in the frame |
| **Glasses, hearing aids or medical devices** | They can check for glare and shadows and know which items are allowed |
| **Religious head coverings** | They can light the face evenly so the hairline and face edges stay visible |
| **A tight travel date** | A rejected photo can delay an application by weeks |
| **Canadian passports** | Canada requires a commercial photographer (see below) |
| **Unusual sizes or several countries at once** | A studio can shoot once and print sizes for each document |

If none of these apply to you, our guide to [places that take passport photos nearby](/tool/passport-photos-near-me) covers the quicker and usually cheaper options.

## What a good passport photo studio does differently

### Lighting and backdrop

The most common reasons for rejection are shadows on the face or background, uneven light and a background that is not plain. A studio uses two or more lights and a proper plain backdrop, so the face is evenly lit and the background carries no shadow. That is hard to copy at home and is the main thing you pay for.

### Measuring the head in the frame

Each country sets how much of the photo your head must fill. For a US passport it is 1 to 1 3/8 inches (25–35 mm) from chin to top of hair. For a Canadian passport it is 31–36 mm. A good studio crops to that measurement, not by eye.

### Prints and a digital file

Ask whether the price includes a digital file as well as prints. You need the file if you plan to renew online or apply for a visa that asks for an upload.

### Retouching: why less is more

This is where some studios get it wrong. Portrait photographers are used to smoothing skin and removing blemishes, but passport authorities reject edited photos:

- The US State Department says: "Do not change your photo using computer software, phone apps or filters," and checks every photo to make sure it was not digitally enhanced or altered.
- The UK requires photos that are "unaltered by computer software."

Ask the studio to supply the photo unretouched. Cropping and sizing are fine. Changing your face, skin or background is not.

## Canada: you must use a commercial photographer

Canada is the main country where using a professional is a requirement rather than a preference. The Government of Canada says to "go to a commercial photographer or photo studio to have your photo taken," and home or self-timer photos are not accepted.

For paper applications, the photographer writes the date the photo was taken and the studio's name and full address on the back of one of the two photos. For online renewals you upload a digital photo, which must still be taken by a commercial photographer, and you enter the studio's details and the date. Canadian photos are 50 × 70 mm, larger than US or European sizes, so say you need a Canadian passport photo when you book.

## How to choose a compliant photographer

### Checklist

- They ask which country and document the photo is for before they start
- They use a plain backdrop and more than one light
- They show you the photo before printing and retake it if your eyes are half closed or there is glare
- They do not retouch your face
- They can give you both prints and a digital file

### Red flags

- They offer to "fix" your skin, remove glasses glare digitally or replace the background in software
- They cannot tell you the head-size range for your document
- Only one size is available, so you would have to trim prints yourself
- No retake if the photo is rejected

### Questions to ask before you book

1. Do you take passport photos for my country and document type?
2. Is a digital file included, and in what format?
3. Do you retouch passport photos? (The answer you want is no.)
4. What happens if my photo is rejected?
5. For a baby: how do you handle infants, and can I book a quiet time?

## Mobile and on-site photographers

Some photographers come to you, with portable lights and a backdrop. This is useful for older relatives who cannot travel easily, people with disabilities, groups such as families or staff teams, and newborns. Expect a call-out fee on top of the photo price. Ask the same compliance questions as you would for a studio.

## How much passport photographers charge

Studio prices vary a lot by city and by what is included, so we do not list them. Expect to pay more than at a pharmacy or big-box store, with extra charges for digital files, extra prints, several sizes or a home visit. A reshoot is usually free if the photo is rejected. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a professional photographer for a US passport photo?

No. The US State Department accepts photos taken anywhere as long as they meet every rule, including size, background, lighting and no digital alteration. A professional is a convenience, not a requirement.

### Can a photographer edit my passport photo to make me look better?

They should not. The US, UK and many other countries reject photos that have been retouched or altered with software. Cropping the photo to the right size is fine; smoothing skin or changing the background is not.

### Is a studio better for a baby's passport photo?

Often, yes. A studio has the time, backdrop and lighting to get a usable photo of a baby, and you avoid holding the baby in the frame. Our [baby and newborn photo guide](/tool/infant-newborn-passport-photo) explains the rules and tips for doing it yourself.

### Should I bring anything to the studio?

Bring the details of the document you are applying for, especially if it is for another country, so the studio can use the right size. Wear something that contrasts with a white background, and leave off glasses unless the rules for your document allow them.
